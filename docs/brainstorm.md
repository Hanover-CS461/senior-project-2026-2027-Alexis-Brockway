# Senior Project Brainstorm — Summary

**Context:** CS senior project, double major in English / Creative Writing.
**Timeline:** August 25 → December (roughly 10–12 real build weeks, given finals).

## What got ruled out
- Anything that *writes stories* or generates fiction (AI story generators, etc.) — "not the vibe."
- The user wants something "English as a whole": reading, analysis, editing, publishing — not generation.

## Earlier candidate directions (for reference)
- Close-reading instrument (motif tracing across a text)
- Echo finder / intertextuality tool
- Draft archaeologist (compare versions of a text)
- Stylometry / authorship fingerprint kit
- Poetry scansion / prosody analyzer

## Idea 1 — Lit journal submission manager (Submittable replacement)
The school's lit journal currently uses Submittable, and the price is becoming too much for the club. Build a replacement tailored to *their* workflow.

Key points:
- Build for **one club**, not a platform → hardcode their settings, only build what they actually use. That's the whole trick for finishing on time.
- Scope strategy: sit down with the club and go through Submittable feature-by-feature ("what do you click every week? what do you never touch?"). Cut everything unused — probably fees/payments, contests, grants, multi-reader scoring, etc.
- Editor-side core: submission intake, a shared queue, statuses, per-editor notes, accept/reject with the journal's letter templates.
- Real client = real urgency, but also real responsibility: **who maintains it after December / after graduation?** Needs a simple, stable, documented handoff.
- Next steps: (1) get the "what do you actually click in Submittable" checklist from the club; (2) ask the advisor about budget and what they'd need to trust the replacement.

## Idea 2 — Close-reading room for the English department
A classroom tool where a professor uploads a text and students annotate it (highlights, marginal notes, tags). The professor sees the whole class's observations bloom into a map — which lines people noticed, which motifs recur, where readers agree or clash.

Key points:
- Built around the user's own knowledge of literary analysis — the tag vocabulary and prompts are the product design.
- CS side: student/instructor accounts, annotation engine, motif map, instructor dashboard.
- Real client = one professor willing to try it in a course.
- Lower stakes than Idea 1: optional for everyone, so more room to experiment, but less urgency and less "glory."

## Idea 3 — Scansion instrument (third shade)
A tool that analyzes a poem's meter and visualizes its rhythm as a visual pulse. No client, no users — a personal, beautiful instrument. The opposite of the other two.

## The choice framing
Three flavors of three months:
1. **Depended on** — the lit journal (real need, real pressure, real responsibility)
2. **Invited in** — the close-reading room (real client, lower stakes)
3. **Alone with the poems** — the scansion instrument (personal, no users)
